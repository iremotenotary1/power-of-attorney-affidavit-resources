Powers of attorney & affidavits

Power of Attorney & Affidavit Resources

This educational resource center helps you understand powers of attorney, affidavits, acknowledgments, jurats, oaths, affirmations, and related execution questions—including documents signed while outside the United States—before you confirm requirements with the receiving party.

General educational information only. This is not legal advice. A notary verifies identity and performs the requested notarial act but does not draft the document, choose a power-of-attorney type, interpret an agent’s authority, or determine legal sufficiency. Not every affidavit or power of attorney requires notarization. Requirements depend on the governing law and receiving party.

Document topics to review first

Use these topic cards as orientation, not as a decision tree. Confirm the document’s execution and acceptance requirements with the recipient or a qualified attorney when appropriate.

General and limited powers of attorney

A power of attorney may authorize an agent or attorney-in-fact to act for a principal. Scope, duration, and acceptance rules vary by governing law and the receiving institution.

Durable and financial powers of attorney

Some forms continue during incapacity or address banking and property matters. The notary does not select the form or decide whether a particular grant of authority is sufficient.

Affidavits and sworn statements

An affidavit is typically a written statement made under oath or affirmation. Whether notarization is required depends on the form, recipient, and controlling procedure.

Jurats, oaths and affirmations

Acknowledgments and jurats are different notarial acts. An oath or affirmation may be required for some sworn statements; other declarations under penalty of perjury may not require notarization.

International and overseas execution

Documents signed abroad may involve embassy appointments, remote online notarization considerations, apostille or legalization steps, and recipient acceptance questions.

Real-estate and representative-capacity documents

Closings, title companies, and recording offices may impose witness, certificate, electronic-signature, and representative-capacity requirements that go beyond a general notarial seal.

Before signing checklist

Preparation reduces delays. Keep the document unsigned until the notarial session unless the recipient’s instructions clearly say otherwise.

  • Keep documents unsigned before the session unless the receiving party provides different written instructions.
  • Confirm the correct signer, including any representative capacity that may appear on the form.
  • Confirm witness requirements and whether witnesses must appear with the signer.
  • Confirm whether the certificate calls for an acknowledgment, a jurat, or another act.
  • Confirm electronic or remote online notarization acceptance with the recipient.
  • Prepare valid identification that matches the name on the document.
  • Obtain recipient or attorney instructions when the form, venue, or capacity language is unclear.

These terms are not interchangeable

People often mix these labels. The certificate language, governing law, and receiving party determine which concept applies.

Power of attorney

A grant of authority from a principal to an agent or attorney-in-fact. It is a document type, not a notarial act by itself.

Affidavit

A written statement offered under oath or affirmation. It may require a jurat or similar ceremony depending on the form.

Acknowledgment

A notarial act in which the signer confirms that the signature is theirs and was made willingly for the purposes stated.

Jurat

A notarial act in which the signer swears or affirms that the contents of a statement are true, usually while signing before the notary.

Unsworn declaration

Some laws allow declarations under penalty of perjury without a notary. Those procedures are not the same as a notarized affidavit.

Witness, certificate, electronic-signature, recording, and recipient-acceptance requirements vary. Confirm them before booking a session.
